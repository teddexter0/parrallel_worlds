# Parallel Worlds playability review

| Area | What I found | What changed in the local build |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cover and intro | The browser loading screen and title art used multiverse graphics that gave a campus thriller the visual language of a supernatural game. The opening repeated each text through narration, timed pauses, and a notification. | The loading screen, title screen, and opening room share a new night-desk scene. Four dialogue beats lead to the first decision. |
| Text and touch | Dialogue sat at a fixed offset inside a fixed-height box. The choice list had no scroll behavior and desktop and touch layouts used different placements. | Dialogue grows with the text. Choice rows wrap and scroll within a capped panel, and the touch variant uses larger type and targets. |
| Choices, feedback and pacing | The opening decision already updates relationships, traits and a named memory. Insight popups required two presses, and hundreds of pauses interrupted dialogue already controlled by the player. | All 21 insight popups advance with one press. I removed 264 waits between dialogue and decision beats across the four acts. Records closes directly back to the current decision or finale. |
| Pause and saves | The HUD exposed Records but had no visible pause. Title buttons linked to settings and load screens that the game's own UI did not define. | The HUD exposes Pause and Records. Pause offers resume, save, load, settings, and return to title. The title offers Continue when a save is present. |
| Email | The existing local source had removed the simulated account gate, but the archive still contained a compiled referral module without a matching source file. | The packaged game removes that module and uses the local profile. Email remains part of the story where characters receive messages. |
| Asset credits | The runtime art included placeholder illustrations. The source tree provides no third-party image credits for them. | In-game credits identify the generated cover, procedural art, synthesized audio, [Eren Arıcı’s café photo](https://www.pexels.com/photo/interior-of-a-cafe-with-a-glass-wall-20281791/), and [Ken Mwaura’s Nairobi skyline photo](https://www.pexels.com/photo/stunning-nairobi-skyline-at-dusk-29069329/). The photos are cropped and color-graded under the [Pexels license](https://www.pexels.com/license/). |
| Audio | The city and café cues were dominated by repetitive tones with little sense of place. | New city ambience uses distant road wash, the café uses layered murmur and cup sounds, and the suspense cue uses a pulse and sparse keys. Settings exposes separate music and effects sliders. |

Static checks covered 119 story labels, 117 unique literal jump and call targets, 25 decision menus, six referenced sound files, and the contents and integrity of both web ZIPs. Source checks establish that branch targets and files exist. They do not establish how each route feels or whether every runtime interaction works.

The Ren’Py runner exercised the opening choice, Pause, Settings, Records, and all six branches of the ending resolver. This establishes the tested behavior in the engine. Complete journeys through all six endings, migration of existing browser saves, audio balance by ear, and physical phone accessibility are outside the verified results. The browser build supports landscape play on phones. The remaining scene and character illustrations are procedural.
